Scope and Purpose of the Policy

Edify is committed to ensuring the safety and well-being of students, staff, volunteers, and partners. We have adopted this safeguarding policy in order to protect children and adults at risk from all forms of abuse and harm, enabling them to live with dignity and respect. Edify unequivocally endorses a culture of zero tolerance toward all forms of abuse and harm, both online and in-person, and seeks that all Edify staff and partners inflict no harm while carrying out their duties with Edify. Edify will do its utmost to ensure that safe environments are created and maintained for every individual who engages with Edify.

Edify staff regularly visit partner schools and attend community events. Having an agreed-upon expectation of conduct is an effective way of not only ensuring the safety of children and adults at risk, but also reducing allegations against Edify staff and partners. We seek to work in ways that reduce any risk to children and adults at risk and minimize the opportunity for harmful behavior to occur in the first place.

This safeguarding policy shall be available in all Edify offices for visitors and staff.

To Whom This Policy Applies

This policy applies to Edify staff and partners, including

All staff – national and international

All volunteers and interns

All contractors – including but not limited to consultants, photographers, and journalists

All board members

All supporters – including but not limited to donors, guest visitors, church groups, and ambassadors

Definition of Safeguarding

According to the International Safety Organisation, “In the NGO sector in general, we understand it to mean taking all reasonable steps to prevent harm, particularly sexual exploitation, abuse and harassment from occurring; to protect people, especially vulnerable adults and children, from that harm; and to respond appropriately when harm does occur.”

Safeguarding Categories

It is important to be aware of three different safeguarding categories within Edify:

Category 1: This refers to issues that directly concern an Edify staff member or representative. Edify will be required to respond to allegations in this category by carrying out an investigation. This category also refers to incidents that arise at Edify-organized events (e.g., youth camps), even if Edify staff or representatives are not directly involved.

Category 2: This refers to issues that concern a staff member of an Edify partner. Edify believes that the partner organization has the duty to respond to the allegation and carry out an investigation, which Edify will require if we are to consider continuing to partner with the organization. The findings of the partner organization’s final investigation report will be shared with Edify.

Category 3: This refers to abuse that happens in a school where Edify or an Edify partner is working. Although Edify or the partner organization does not have formal responsibility to investigate, Edify or the partner organization may have influence in the school and has to decide if we continue to partner with that school. Each situation should be handled on a case-by-case basis to ensure that reporting and follow up is done in a way that is in the best interest of the child or adult at risk.

Additionally, Edify staff should be aware of another important safeguarding area they may encounter in the course of their work with Edify:

Community Incidents: This refers to safeguarding incidents that occur at non-partner schools and in the surrounding community. Edify does not have direct responsibility or control, but as staff who are aware of these issues, we can challenge the situation and help change it.

Reporting and Responding (Please see Appendix B for reporting process)

Edify partners with schools and we have been ‘invited’ by the school to be on their campus. In maintaining our protection of children and adults at risk, staff are required to report abuse, suspected abuse, or neglect. An Edify staff member or partner may suspect abuse by noting signs of abuse, being told of abuse, or witnessing conduct that is of concern.

Do not hesitate to report abuse, and you should do so immediately (no later than 24 hours of becoming aware of the allegation or incident of concern). When you report what you have seen or heard, you may help someone stay safe.

If you have noticed the warning signs of abuse and neglect, the first step is to make sure the victim is safe and has access to necessary care. Please be sure to immediately inform your Country Director, who in turn will inform the Regional VP. A Plan of Action will then be developed. If you suspect the Country Director of abuse, then immediately inform the Regional VP. If you do not report your suspicions, the abuse might continue.

You should have a reasonable suspicion of the abuse, but you do not have to "prove" the abuse or be positive that it occurred. If you have observed any contact between a child/adult at risk and an Edify staff/volunteer/contractor in a context where they should not be interacting, or if you have any concerns that these relations are not healthy, please report this immediately (within 24 hours).

Please document what you have seen. The Suspected Abuse/Neglect Reporting Form may be used to document details of abuse, suspected abuse, inappropriate behavior, or non-abuse policy violations. As soon as this report is complete give a copy to the Edify Country Director (as soon as possible- maximum 24 hours). All persons involved in reporting or documenting any alleged incident of abuse should hold this information in confidence.

If a confidential disclosure is made by an individual (who is not a child) about an allegation of abuse or sexual harassment AND there is no immediate risk of serious harm, a report should only be made with that individual’s consent. In such instances, guidance may also be sought from the Country Director or Regional VP without disclosing the names or identifying information of the individuals affected. Seeking such guidance is strongly encouraged.

Checklist for Reporting and Responding to Abuse

If you see a safeguarding concern or someone reports and incident to you, please do the following:

Intervene if the child or adult at risk is in immediate danger (ideally bringing another adult with you); if you feel that you would be placing yourself in danger by intervening, immediately contact a police officer or other authority to engage the situation

Immediately arrange for appropriate care (ambulance, first aid, etc.) if an injury is involved

Note down the key information on Edify’s Reporting Form once the child/adult at risk is no longer in immediate danger

Give the form to the appropriate Edify leader (e.g., Country Director, Safeguarding Officer) immediately, or as soon as you are able to do so

Procedures for Partner Schools to Report Allegations Against Edify Staff and Their Guests

When Edify engages in a new school partnership, it should be made explicit to the school proprietor/director that Edify staff, and any of their guests, will follow all school policies relating to outside visitors. Country Directors will communicate to the proprietor/director that if they suspect an Edify staff member or guest of inappropriate behavior, then the proprietor/director should immediately inform the Edify Country Director.

Safeguarding Committee

A Safeguarding Committee will review all safeguarding incidents on a quarterly basis. A Safeguarding Incident Log will be kept and reviewed quarterly by the Committee. The Committee will pass along any key incidents to the CEO/COO/VP of People for their review and input. Edify is committed to following all government requirements when it comes to reporting incidents to regulatory and government bodies. If there is evidence that criminal activity may be involved in the incident, the incident will be reported to the relevant child protection and/or police authorities in the country where the incident happened, along with the country of origin of the accused (as appropriate). This will be carried out as long as it is in the best interest of the child or adult at risk.

Expectations of Partner Organizations

All Edify partner schools, alliances, agencies, and other partner organizations that work with Edify are expected to consistently implement strong safeguarding practices in all activity areas that involve children and adults at risk.

Safeguarding and Media

The safety and dignity of children and adults at risk will be upheld at all times through Edify’s media and communications content. All personal information and details of children or adults at risk will not be used if it increases the potential for harm or injury to them or their families.

Code of Conduct

Those who accept the responsibility of working with children and adults at risk at Edify partner schools, and at related activities, agree to the following:

Treat children and adults at risk with respect and dignity;

Comply with Edify’s Safeguarding Policy;

Never physically, sexually, verbally, or emotionally harm/neglect any child or adult at risk whom Edify serves;

Do my best in all situations to prevent any child or adult at risk from experiencing harm physically, verbally, sexually, or emotionally;

Ensure that when I am with a child, I follow the two-adult rule at all times so as not to be alone with a child;

Never provide my personal contact details (e.g., email, mobile number, postal address) to a child or young person, nor contact them via a personal social media account; and

Immediately (within 24 hours) report any concerns I have about a child’s welfare.

Guidelines on Personal Conduct

Common expressions of affection (e.g., hugs), affirmation (e.g., pats on the back, high fives), and blessings (e.g., laying on of hands) when done without sexual connotations, are appropriate in this community of caring Christians.

Expressions of affection shall not be excessive or imposed upon others. No child or adult should be made to feel guilty if they do not wish to participate in these expressions of affection. Children and adults will be made aware that they are free to participate or not, as they feel comfortable.

Adults shall not touch or interact with children in any way that is intended to be sexually stimulating. Any activity of this type is illegal, as well as a violation of trust.

Adults shall not engage in inappropriate behavior with children. Examples include but are not limited to: seeking private time with children, taking children on outings or overnight trips without other adults, swearing in front of children, sharing sexually suggestive or explicit content with children, or making suggestive comments to children.

Corporal punishment or verbal abuse is never permitted.

Guidelines for Visiting Schools

When possible, inform the school in advance of your visit.

Wear either Edify attire (e.g., shirt with Edify’s logo) and/or a name tag that identifies you as an Edify employee.

Sign in at the school’s main office upon arrival and inform school staff as to your purpose and with whom you will be meeting.

If you are interacting with children, be sure that another adult is present at all times.

Only use designed staff restrooms when visiting schools and do not enter student restrooms.

Prior to taking any photos or videos of children, ask permission from the school. If the photos/videos are for public use, formal written permission is required and should include the stated intent for the photos/videos.

If you believe there is evidence of abuse, the school administration should be informed by the Country Director. Through our leadership trainings, Edify is encouraging all partner schools to have a Child Protection Policy. If the school has such a policy, staff should be aware of the school’s processes for reporting incidents. has such a policy, staff should be aware of the school’s processes for reporting incidents.

If an Edify staff member believes the school administration is abusing children, then the Country Director should be informed immediately (within 24 hours).

Power and Positions of Trust

A relationship between an adult and a child/adult at risk is not a relationship between equals. There is potential for exploitation and harm of vulnerable people. Adults therefore have a responsibility to ensure that an unequal balance of power is not used for personal advantage or gratification.

Adults should always maintain appropriate professional boundaries and avoid behavior that might be misinterpreted by others. They should report and record any incident with this potential.

Safeguarding Procedures

Screening and Background Checks

All Edify country offices will have a screening/background check process in place for all new employees. When possible, all Edify employees, prior to employment, will undergo a criminal background check. Updated criminal background checks will be performed periodically, as deemed appropriate, usually every 5 years.

Commitment to Policy

All new Edify staff will be required to sign the Safeguarding Policy Statement indicating their acceptance of and willingness to live in alignment with the policy.

Training

All Edify employees will also participate in an annual safeguarding training provided by Edify. The responsibility for ensuring the Safeguarding Policy is included as part of new staff orientation programs shall rest with each Country Director.

Appendix A: Signs and Types of Abuse

Signs of Child Abuse

One step in helping abused or neglected children and adults at risk is to recognize the warning signs. Report any suspected physical abuse, medical neglect, mental abuse, or sexual abuse inflicted upon a child by caretakers. No one symptom alone proves child abuse, but when they appear often, or in combination with other symptoms, adults need to report them.

It may be physical abuse when you see a child with:

● Questionable burns, bites, bruises, broken bones, welts or black eyes ● Fading bruises or other marks after an absence from school

It may be physical abuse when the school staff member, parent or other caregiver:

• Offers an unconvincing explanation for an injury

• Describes the child as "bad" or "stupid”

• Uses harsh physical discipline

It may be physical neglect when a child:

• Steals food or money from classmates, often seems hungry

• Lacks medical or dental care

• Often appears dirty or tired

It may be physical neglect when a school staff member, parent or caregiver:

• Abuses alcohol or drugs

• Appears indifferent to a child's needs

• Seems apathetic or depressed

• Consistently fails to keep important appointments or to take medicines

It may be emotional abuse or neglect when the child:

Displays self-destructive behavior, like cutting or burning himself/herself

Becomes overly compliant and passive or extremely demanding and aggressive

Acts inappropriately adult-like (such as parenting other children) or inappropriately infantile (such as frequent rocking or head banging)

Can't make or keep friends

It may be emotional abuse or neglect when the school staff member or caregiver:

Constantly puts down or blames child

Rejects the child outright

Makes unreasonable demands on the child without regard to his or her abilities or developmental level

Gets defensive or refuses to consider help for the child’s school problems

It may be sexual abuse when the child:

Can't walk or sit easily

Displays unusual sexual knowledge or behavior, such as being seductive

Withdraws, seems depressed, or can't get along with peers

Abuses drugs or alcohol

Has unexplained money or gifts

Expresses thoughts of suicide and low self-worth

Relationships in Which Abuse May Occur:

Between an adult (the abuser) and a vulnerable adult

Between an adult (the abuser) and a child

Between a child (the abuser) and another child

Between a child (the abuser) and a vulnerable adult

Types of Abuse

Physical Abuse: this may involve forms of physical harm such as hitting, throwing, shaking, burning/scalding, poisoning, suffocating, drowning, or otherwise causing physical harm. This includes fabricating symptoms of or intentionally causing an illness.

Emotional Abuse: this involves persistent emotional mistreatment, such as causing severe and ongoing adverse effects on emotional development. Emotional abuse may involve communicating to a child or adult at risk that they are inadequate, unloved, worthless, or only valued only for their ability to meet the needs of others. It may also involve the imposition of age/developmentally inappropriate expectations, or preventing a child from engaging in normal interactions at school. Emotional abuse might also include a child seeing/hearing another person being ill-treated, causing someone to feel frequently in danger or afraid, or exploiting/corrupting a child or adult at risk.

Sexual Abuse: this involves enticing or forcing a child or adult at risk to engage in sexual activities, including prostitution, regardless of if the child is aware of what is taking place. Sexual abuse may involve physical contact and penetrative or non-penetrative sexual acts. It may also include the involvement of children in viewing or producing pornographic material, or encouraging children to act in a sexually inappropriate manner.

Neglect: this is the ongoing failure to meet the basic physical/psychological needs of a child or adult at risk, such as providing sufficient clothing and food, shelter, supervision (including inadequate caretakers), access to relevant medical treatment and care. It also may include failure to protect from physical or emotional harm. Neglect may also entail unresponsiveness to or neglect of fundamental emotional needs.

Child Sexual Exploitation: this involves sexual abuse in which a child or adult at risk is exploited for power, money, or status. Typically the abuser is someone known to the child.

Appendix B — Reporting Process/Flow

  1. Immediate

    A child or adult at risk has disclosed concerning information to you, or you have witnessed or received a report of an incident or complaint or rumor involving the behavior of an adult in relation to a child/adult at risk which may be considered abuse or poor practice.

  2. Immediate

    Stay calm, reassure, make no promises, ask a few questions, follow this guide.

  3. Immediate

    Is the victim in need of urgent medical attention?

  4. Within 1 hour

    If yes: Contact emergency services; inform of potential safeguarding issue.

  5. Within 12 hours

    Report to Country Director & Edify Regional Human Resource Director (or if not available Global safeguarding officer/ HR Director) at earliest opportunity and at the latest within 12 hours. If CD not available, report to RVP.

  6. As soon as possible

    Does the allegation or incident involve the child’s parents or guardian or caregiver? If no, the CD/ Safeguarding officer informs parents, guardians, or caregivers at the earliest opportunity.

  7. Within 24 hours

    CD and Safeguarding officer inform RVP and COO/ CEO respectively: Category 3 incidents reported to Board by CEO.

  8. Within 48 hours

    CD/ Safeguarding officer calls a meeting with the Safeguarding Committee to discuss a plan within 48 hours. Safeguarding Committee will include COO, VP for People, relevant RVP and VP for CCE unless any are somehow connected to the case.

  9. Written record

    Whatever the circumstances, the person receiving and then reporting a complaint or incident must record a written statement, preferably using Edify‘s incident report form. This will be submitted to the Edify safeguarding committee at the earliest opportunity.

  10. Ongoing

    The Safeguarding Committee lead keeps the CEO updated on the situation. The CEO keeps the board informed of status and progress.
