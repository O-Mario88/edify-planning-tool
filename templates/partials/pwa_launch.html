{% load static %} {% comment %} The launch screen the installed app shows while it boots. It exists because the operating system's own splash ends the moment the document starts painting, which is well before the shell is usable — the app appeared to flash a half-drawn page. This overlay continues that splash into the product's own brand moment: the same navy family as the manifest's `background_color`, deepening toward the edges, with the sidebar's wordmark lockup centred — so the handover from the OS screen reads as the app arriving, not as two unrelated splashes. The composition is the brand plate: the wordmark and product name centred on a deep navy field lit from the upper right, a quiet field of dots rising from the bottom of the screen, and one small sign of life beneath the lockup. That sign adapts to the screen it is on: phones show a turning ring over "Loading your workspace…", larger screens a hairline progress track with a travelling highlight over "Preparing your dashboard…". Both are rhythm, not measurement — neither pretends to know how far boot has come. Standalone only, and at every launch — but only at launches. In a browser tab there is no OS splash to continue, and an overlay there would just be a delay. A launch is recognised by how the document arrived: opening the app from its icon navigates with no referrer, while every link followed inside the running app is referred by the page before it. Gating on the referrer rather than a once-per-session flag means closing the app and opening it again always replays the splash — sessionStorage could not promise that, because browsers restore it when they restore the app after killing it in the background. The markup and its critical CSS are inline on purpose. A launch screen that waits for a stylesheet is not a launch screen — it has to paint on the first frame, before main.css has been fetched. The wordmark image is the same asset the sidebar paints next, so by the time the overlay lifts it is already warm in the cache. {% endcomment %}
